## The rows

Each line has what the mechanism is for, and the log's entry that weighed its simpler form.

### Between nodes

- **1, the exchange.** A spawn or a compute carries the definitions a peer lacks. Code with every message, hashes for verification alone and resolution at the first call were refused (*What the Design Notes Argued*, *Code Travels Only With a Spawn*).
- **2, a node's telling and the pick.** Above.
- **3, the carrier.** It is the host's distribution with Ernest's keys and floor, taken in place of a protocol of Ernest's own, and the host's buffer in place of a limit of Ernest's (*The Nodes' Reasons, Kept*).
- **4, the gateway and the frames.** One gateway a node, since a gateway for each peer would miss a find's frame. A message carries no hash and passes no gateway (*The Nodes' Reasons, Kept*).
- **12, a reload of `ernest.conf`.** A node's peers change while it runs, at a hangup (*A Node's End and Its Reload*).
- **14, what a node holds of code.** A unit is held for the node's life. A lazy node was refused, and a purge is described and not built (*Lean at the Limits*, *A Purge Needs Sight*).
- **15, a node's end told.** A frame, since the host does not show an end from a death, narrowed on 2026-11-20 (*MVP 3.1 Read*, *A Node's End Waits for No Send*).
- **25, a compute.** A call on a peer, as the host's `erpc:call` (*Placing Work by Name*).
- **36, the launch's tag.** An adapted address of an earlier start is dead. The log has no entry of its own for it. It is 16 lines, and follows from row 11.

### The runtime's own processes

- **5, the system processes.** Threading a value through every function that prints was refused (*Ambient Sys, Five Principles*).
- **7, a full table and open files.** Its 1,167 words are what a user meets at a limit of the host's, in 97 lines of code. Lower defaults and a reserve were refused (*A Node's Limits*, *The Carrier's Acceptor at a Full Table*).
- **17, the record of a waiting call.** A restart that ends no call breaks §6.9, and a callee that keeps its own replies leaves a request with no owner (*The Sitting on §8.6's Bookkeeping*).
- **19, a restart a supervisor asks.** A child as a new process leaves every service binding with a dead address, and a kill loses the address the restart exists to keep (*A Sibling Restarts at Its Next Wait*).
- **22, a resource's owner.** A `finally` or a `defer` was refused as a second way (*Who Owns a Process*, *A Process Owns What It Opens*).
- **26, the reaper.** A process that registers itself has an end nobody sees, and a spawn's wait is its back pressure: eight spawning processes took 52 seconds without it and 0.74 with it (*Supervision Stays in the Reaper*, *Spawn Keeps Its Wait*).
- **31, the killer.** No process can name it, so no two processes suspend each other, and a kill needs no room in a full table (*The Sitting on §8.6's Bookkeeping*, *A Node's Limits*).
- **34, what a restart cancels.** A restart is a new run in all but its address (*A Restart Begins Afresh*).
- **35, `Process.run`'s second process.** The runner answering its caller races (*A Worker's Value or Its End Built*).
- **37, the supervisor's two processes.** Written in Ernest. Children as new processes behind forwarders were refused (*A `Supervisor` in the Standard Library, and `fault`*, *The `Supervisor`'s Shape*).

### The end

- **13, the deadlock look.** Above.
- **The end's flush and its cut**, 496 of row 9's 973 words and five of its eight items, its code not counted apart. An end with no draining loses a program's last line. No bound in time was weighed, since it would be a time the runtime chose (§0). Each of its corners was answered by a narrower sentence and no mechanism (*Four "Partly Resolved" Tails: Blocks, Reply Timing, Termination, Deadlock Scope*, *A Termination Ends a Stalled Flush*, *The Job Halts Without the Host's Flush*).

### The toolchain and the shell

- **6, a load's evaluator.** A load ran in its caller until *The Argument's Four Decisions*, at the cost of one process a load.
- **16, a session's screen.** Reads routed by session were refused as a second mechanism (*The Attached Shell's Sentences*).
- **21, a fault's trace.** Every fault carries one (*Every Fault Carries a Trace*).
- **23, the lock on `ernest.pid`.** A record lock, over a comparison of paths and over `flock` (*MVP 3.1 Read*).
- **28, the runtime's own lines.** It keeps no promise of the report's, as its row says. The log weighs how its lines are written and not whether it is a process. It is 169 lines.
- **30, a capture's collector.** A runner that shows output beneath a failed line was refused, since a test still cannot read the line (*A Case's Standard Error*).

### Input and output

- **20, the terminal's claim.** Keys and lines cannot both be read (*`Keys`, and Who Owns the Terminal*).
- **27, a socket's writer and closer.** One closer for all was refused, since the host's close over TLS waits ten seconds (*A Node's Own Processes at a Full Table*).
- **29, a listener's workers.** Measured against the host alone and kept (*`Tcp`, Measured, and the Refusals Gone*). The simpler way for a worker to learn its listener's end is MVP 4.5's item 155.
- **32, the helper `ern_exec`.** A shell wrapper cannot tell a failure to start from the program's own status (*`Os.run` Runs Through a Helper in C*).
