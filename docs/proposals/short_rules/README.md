## Why

The root's `CLAUDE.md` grew from 4,113 words and 51 rules on 2026-10-21 to 6,206 words and 84 rules on 2026-11-19. Each rule was written after a mistake. Most of them say how to think, and a session that weighs 84 of them follows the strictest reading of the ones it remembers.

On 2026-11-19 that showed. MVP 4.16's item 120 took about three hours for a fix of 68 lines, most of it probing a cause that the code showed in a few minutes. From 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-19 the plan numbered 72 new items and closed about 66, and its list of items left grew from 23 to 29.

## What the draft keeps

About 1,300 words and 46 rules. It keeps what a session cannot work out for itself:

- what is the user's: the checkout, the index, untracked files, the push;
- who decides: the report first, and the user for the language;
- what the user wants of Ernest, which is taste;
- how a result is reported.

It also keeps one line against each known fault of a session's own judgment. A session works around a gap, reports a thing done too early, decides what is the user's to decide, and probes long past the point of use. Two rules are new: work in proportion, and what stands before the launch.

## What the draft cuts

**Moved to the document its job reads.** Each needs its sentences placed there before the root's file is replaced:

- a proposal's life and a design note's form, to `docs/proposals/` and `docs/design/`;
- the template and its copies, to `docs/clean_room.md`;
- a flake's hunt, a timing test under load, a long run's first step and the table of refusals, to `docs/development.md`;
- the glossary, Appendix F, the paragraph's length and a shell session's cuts, to the two style documents;
- memory that no collection reclaims, to `docs/memory_checks.md`.

**Held by a test already.** The test says so when it is broken: the paragraph's ceiling, the spelling, user-facing text without the project's timeline, each report file's third line, the form of `CHANGES.md`.

**Left to judgment, and lost as text.** These are the cost. Some of the mistakes they were written against will come back:

- the checklist that closes an item, and the two searches of its read-back;
- an item read against the items left;
- one issue at a time;
- a defense that names its attacker, and options before defenses;
- a library function weighed on E.0, which E.0 itself states;
- the session's length, its effort and when to clear it.
