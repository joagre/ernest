## The findings, most serious first

**P1. A termination subscription outlives a restart; everything else a process asked the runtime for does not.** §6.9: "What the process has asked the runtime for is cancelled, whenever it asked, before its first run or in any run: its alarms (Appendix E.15), its monitors, and its subscriptions to the terminal (§8.2) and to faults (Appendix E.21)." E.23: "`terminating(wrap)` subscribes the calling process to the program's end as `Process.faults` subscribes to faults (E.21) ... A subscription is one per process, the latest, and ends with its process", and E.21 has a faults subscription end "when its process dies or restarts (§6.9)". The log (*Termination Told, Built*): "A restart does not end it, since E.23 ends a subscription with its process alone, unlike §6.9's subscription to faults", which restates the rule in place of a reason. Principle 1: the reader of §6.9 predicts that a restart cancels what the process asked the runtime for, and E.23's analogy to `Process.faults` predicts it a second time; the departure is stated only by the subscription's absence from §6.9's list and by "ends with its process", and E.23's own comparison says the opposite. Principle 2: two subscriptions of one shape, shape rule 8's "one that delivers later", have two rules at a restart. §8.6 then carries a clause for the case, "whether the subscriber ends or restarts": a subscriber that restarts while the end waits loses the reply with its mailbox and the end goes on without it, and its surviving subscription is told again only where the restarted run subscribes again, which a new subscription would be too; so the survival buys the one case a subscriber that subscribed before entering its restarting function and restarted before the end, where a faults subscription of the same shape is lost. The principles as written do not admit the opposite: §6.9's own sentence is the rule, and the exception has no argument in the log. Decision: `report`, one rule for every subscription at a restart, in §6.9's sentence or in its list; whichever rule stays, E.23 drops the analogy or states the departure where §6.9's reader meets it.

**Checked and sound:**
- **The bytes.** `term_to_binary(Term, [deterministic, {minor_version, 2}])` hashed with SHA-256: `{ernest_form, 1, Form}` for a definition alone, `{ernest_form, 1, {group, Forms}}` for a group, `{ernest_member, 1, GroupHash, P}` for a member, `{ernest_type, 1, Type}` for a key's type. The chunk is compressed, and the hash is taken of the uncompressed term.
- **Node by node.** Every term of Appendix H's **Definitions**, **References**, **Types**, **Expressions**, **Patterns** and **Supplies** matches the code's term: tags, arity and field order. Restrictions are a sorted list. A foreign scheme is numbered by itself. The prelude and the standard library are referenced by name, the library's own references among them. Libraries under `libs/` are hashed as a program's code: `is_provided` is the prelude's member types plus the standard library's namespaces, which the plan's item 2 states and `library_hashed_test` holds.
- **Groups.** Groups are strongly connected components found by `digraph_utils:condensation`, hashed in dependency order, with members in source order. A definition alone names itself `{in_group, 1}`. Moving a member changes the group's hash. A cycle through bindings alone is refused by the checker; one joins a group only through a type's `compare` (H2).
- **Lambda positions.** For every expression and statement clause, the walk records a lambda or local function's span in the same pre-order in which `within/1` meets it in the closed form. The `{lambda, …}` and `{function, …}` tags cannot be confused with any other term.
- **Literals.** An `Int` of any size has one encoding. A `-0.0` never reaches a form: `ern_parser:negated/1` keeps `0.0`, and a negated expression is `{negate, …}`. A `Char` and an `Int` of one code point differ by their kind. A `String` is UTF-8, and the lexer refuses surrogates. `x` and `x:size(8)-big-unsigned` are one segment.
- **Annotations and requirements.** An annotation's presence is in the form, by design. The written order of `needs` is in the form and matters, since supplies are passed in that order. The numbering does not depend on the checker's variable ids: the same definition after other declarations gives the same form.
- **`derives compare` and order.** A derived `compare` is the checker's elaborated `fn`, placed after its type and hashed with its body. It and a written one share a group with the type. `negate` and the operators are left out, and a changed `compare` changes the type's, the key's and its users' hashes (`type_compare_test`).
- **Abstract types.** An abstract type's constructors and the private types its fields name are in its hash.
- **Pipes.** `piped_call` is tagged by the same predicate the emitter uses to evaluate the first argument before the callee.
- **Identity.** A binding's identity is `{binding, QualifiedName, Hash}` and a function's or type's is `{hash, Hash}`. Two bindings with one initializer have one hash, two identities, and give their dependents two hashes.
- **§11.1's recompile rule.** `ern_build:current/6` compares each referenced hash with the dependency's interface. A build showed a change to a private callee recompiling a dependent that names the changed public function, and not one that names an unchanged function of the same module. The interface's own hash leaves out identities and reaches.
- **The shell.** An input's `$Input<n>` slot is reused only after its module is collected and purged. A reload's previous version, `Counter$1`, keeps the original hashes through the renamed identities.

## Checked and sound

- **Units.** Each version is a unit of its own, `ern@counter$2`, `$3` and so on. The first free name is measured against the host's limit before it becomes an atom (`fresh_unit/2`). Nothing a load or a reload gave is unloaded: through three reloads, the first version's service answered `105` and the last version's `300`.
- **Same code is taken as it is.** A node's build unit of the same code is taken as it is, `held(... "ern@greet$2")` printing `false`. A changed source becomes `ern@other$2`, printing `true`.
- **The units map routes calls.** The emitter's `units` map, through `unit/2`, routes every remote call, remote fun, `'$deps'` entry and member call of a loaded module. The direct `ern_namespace:erlang_module` calls left in the emitter are prelude and standard library ones, which cannot be reloaded. A previous version's members run its own unit: `a + b` on `Coin$1.Coin` gave `Coin(3)` and on the current type `Coin(1003)`, and each version's `compare` orders its own `OrderedSet`. Nested namespaces work too: `Net.Shape$1.Vec`, with `+` and `==` on it.
- **Renaming and the reload's lines.** A changed or removed type is renamed to `Counter$1` in the inputs' and holders' interfaces and in the previous versions'. A type that keeps its hash keeps its name (`k : Net.Shape.Kind`, and `c2` after a reload that left `Msg` alone). The reload names each binding left on a previous version, declared functions included (`the bindings double and v`). A binding made at version 1 of a type that version 2 left unchanged prints as `Keyed$2.Msg`, the type of version 2 by hash, which matches the report's words.
- **Messages across versions.** A message of either version to the other's address is refused with the help: `expected Counter$1.Msg, found Counter.Msg` and the reverse, and `:type` gives it too. A message of the version's own kind, made by a function kept from before, is taken: `send(c, inc(5))` then `105`.
- **What a reload compiles.** A reload compiles again what `ern build` would. A body change to `A.value` recompiled `B`, which calls it, and not `C`. A body change to `A.unused` recompiled `C` alone. An interface change recompiled both.
- **Failed reloads.** A parse error, a type error and a dependent's type error each print `nothing was reloaded`, and the session runs on unchanged (`10 : Int`). A stale user whose source is gone refuses the reload, naming it.
- **First-time modules in a reload.** A module a reload loads for the first time, whose binding faults, refuses the reload and kills and names what the reload started: `H2.z:1 faulted: division by zero; nothing was reloaded, and the process spawned at H1.worker:1 was killed`.
- **Kept values.** Kept values are of one hash only (`kept_values/2`), as soundness.md §7 states.
- **`:load` refusals.** `:load` refuses a module already loaded, and one of the standard library, with the texts the code gives. An `Os.exit` in a binding is reported as `E.x:1 faulted: exited with status 3; nothing was loaded`.
- **The three lifted refusals.** `:load`, `:reload` and a key at a session type all work in a shell that is a node. A key at a session type was offered and found across two shell nodes. The one refusal kept for MVP 3.2 has the exact text of `docs/development.md`'s row, and no stale shell row remains in the table.
- **`checked_interfaces` and `collected`.** `checked_interfaces/1` gives the checker the loaded modules' interfaces with the previous ones. `collected/1` touches session modules alone (`$Input`, `$Bindings`), so no unit a load brought is ever collected.
- **The four tests.** `node_takes_its_build_unit`, `compiled_beside_another`, `reload_keeps_previous_versions` and `reload_keeps_the_type_checked_under` test what their comments say.
- **Entry 97 still holds.** `Counter$1.Inc` cannot be written: `illegal character '$'`. `:browse Counter$1` is refused, and `:doc Counter$1.Msg` answers `no documentation`. After a second change, a binding of `Counter$3.Msg` cannot be sent `Reset`, though its process takes it.
- **Entry 98 still holds.** `:processes` lists two or three versions' services alike, `Counter.service:10` and `Keyed.service:6`, and nothing tells which version a process runs.
