A socket `accept` answers over TLS is the host's before its handshake; the socket's writer runs the handshake, writes wait behind it, reads time out on their own milliseconds, and the socket's process answers a close meanwhile (principle 3). `connectTls` gives the host the name the program wrote as the server name, so `Verified` checks that name, a wildcard the certificate gives matched as a browser matches it (principle 1). TCP's process starts the host's `ssl` once per launch, on a node too, whose carrier runs without it. Measured: a write and a read of 100 bytes over TLS add 26 to 37 microseconds to the host's 71, what the plain socket adds to its 45, the call to the socket's process.

**The TLS linger bound.** E.18 drops what the far end has not taken 5 seconds after the end and resets the connection. Over TLS the host's socket went 15.0 seconds after the close: the host's `ssl` closes with two waits of 5 seconds of its own, for its sender and for the far end's answer, and its close ignores the time it is given. The shim could keep the plain socket and run TLS over it by the host's upgrade, closing the port itself on time; that is a port of the host's `ssl` closed behind its back, a trick, and CLAUDE.md's *Design* has a cost go by needing less and never by a trick. So E.18 states the host's bound over TLS, up to 10 seconds later, and the edge the code shows: a close that goes into the host's queue behind bytes the far end has not taken is sent on until it takes them, which no process stops. The test of the 9-second window stays out.

**The terminal in `Os`, built.** `Os.startTerminal` and `Os.resize` share the one program process `Os.start` has, so `read`, `write`, `kill`, `give` and `closeInput` serve both kinds by one path (principle 5). The helper takes a `terminal` mode with the size on its command line, opens the pseudo-terminal before it forks and makes the child its session's leader with `setsid` and `TIOCSCTTY`; a `w` frame carries a resize, and a `t` frame a terminal the host could not open, answered `Other` and never `NotFound` or `Denied` (principle 1). `closeInput` hangs up at once and drops input not yet taken, as closing a window does (principle 1). `resize` answers from the program's process with no milliseconds (E.0 shape rule 8); `NotATerminal` comes before `Invalid`, and the answer is `Right(Unit)` once nothing is left to size. The helper waits with `select(2)` in both modes, since `poll(2)` serves no device on macOS, which a pseudo-terminal's master is; no Mac ran it, and the helper compiles under clang at its flags.

## The Four Libraries, 2026-11-01

The sections' shapes depart from the proposal's first cut where E.0 and the RFCs asked, each departure here.

**`libs/base64`.** `encode` is a shim of `base64:encode/1` (E.0 rule 1). `decode` is a primitive in `erl/base64`, since the host raises on a text it cannot read. **`libs/crypto`.** `sha1` and `randomBytes`, Ernest over two `foreign fn`s that are `crypto:hash/2` and `crypto:strong_rand_bytes/1` exactly, with no Erlang adapter (E.0 rule 1); `sha1` gives the host its atom through `Foreign.atom`, as `Ets.new` does, and `randomBytes` closes the host's `badarg` on a negative count with `Int.max(count, 0)`, since a count below 0 is 0 (§7.4). **`libs/time`.** `civil`, `fromCivil` and `weekday` are Ernest over four of the host's `calendar` functions, with no Erlang helper, since the host raises nowhere Ernest cannot foresee (E.0 rule 1). `civil` takes the host's floor, which its page states, so a time before the epoch is in the second that begins before it; it drops the millisecond, the calendar's grain being the second, so that `Civil` is six fields and `fromCivil` their inverse at the second's start. `fromCivil` checks the date with `valid_date` and the time of day in Ernest before the host's gregorian seconds, which raise on a 24th hour. The reason phrase is the status's and no field, a response's structure being its version, status and headers.
