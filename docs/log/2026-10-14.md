## Kill Darlings, 2026-10-14

Decided with the user on 2026-10-14, after a reader who knows ML and Erlang asked why `fn foo(a) = send(a, 1)` is `(Address(Int)) -> Unit with e+` and what `with` says. The proposal under `proposals/kill_darlings/` is the specification, its reasons its sections 1 to 5, and this entry says what was decided and why in short. Two things go from the type system, built as MVP 3.26 before 0.4.0's tag.

The reply's obligation goes. A `Reply` answered exactly once on every path had two halves: "at most once" bought nothing, since the runtime drops a second or late answer already; "at least once" caught a forgotten answer when the program was compiled, and that half put `!` on 111 of the standard library's 359 functions, since Ernest has no borrowing and `List.size` on a list of replies drops them as surely as a server that forgets one. The record held no program in which the check caught a mistake of anyone's, and seventeen defects of the check itself. `Reply`, `answer`, `Address.call` and `callForever` stay as they are; a forgotten answer is `None` at the deadline or a fault at the callee's end, and a `callForever` on a callee that lives on waits, stated, as Erlang's `infinity` does. Principle 5 decided, principle 1 agreed, and principle 3's "a rule the value decides is a value, a message, or a fault when it runs" covers it.

The mark on every function that acts goes, and `with` becomes `receiving`. The mark did two jobs, that a function acts and what its mailbox holds, and for a function that only sends the second named a mailbox it never used. A function type names its mailbox where its own mailbox is involved and nowhere else; sending, spawning, answering, calling and every other action carry no mark, as in ML, Erlang and Gleam. The record held no program in which purity checking caught a mistake, and eight defects of the effect checker. Principle 1 decided, the reader who knows ML and Erlang predicting this and tripping on the mark; principle 2, the mark with one job; principle 5 in its own words, no word written for a feature a function does not use; and principle 3 met as it is met for failure, visible where it happens. What goes with it is stated: a signature no longer shows that a function acts, and a guard or a `compare` may send, visible in its own code. The reading that decided found a soundness hole the proposal had not seen, the generalization of a top-level `let` whose initializer is a call, held today only by the process-only rule, and closed it with ML's value restriction, which the same reader predicts; and three rules follow from the findings, `Process.current` for a function that needs its identity and not its mailbox, the process-only restriction kept for a timed wait alone, and `Code.running` asking `Unit` of its function.

Conversations, a process receiving on typed inboxes beside its mailbox, each a tag on the host's one queue, with `Reply` one of them and a clause of `receive` naming its source, were weighed and set aside: a library that must wait on messages of its own is a process and a call, `programs/gather_today.ern`, thirty lines under today's language, so the design would add a concept, a check at every spawn and a form of `receive` to do inside a process what a process and a call already do; principle 5 decided. What would change it is a program the idiom cannot write well, found while writing the tour. The one need it served, moving a backlog of messages to another process, is the library `Mailbox`, two functions over the language as it stands, MVP 3.3's; set aside on 2026-10-20 with the tour's rolling upgrade, its one program, and under *Later* with what would bring it back (*The Tour, Shorter*).

## Clause Bodies All or None, 2026-10-14

Decided with the user on 2026-10-14, to be built as MVP 3.3's first item; on 2026-10-20 made a milestone of its own before the tour, MVP 3.28, since it is the formatter's work and not the tour's (*The Tour, Shorter*). Today `ern format` keeps a clause's body beside its pattern where it fits and puts a block's body below, so one `match` mixes two shapes, and a clause whose body grows into a block changes its pattern's line too. The rule: in a `match` or a `receive`, either every clause's body stands beside its pattern, or every one goes on the line below it, indented, a block's `{` staying after the arrow. All stay beside where every body fits; one that does not, a block or a body past the line's limit, sends them all below.

Each construct then has one shape, its patterns a column to scan, and a body that grows moves its own lines alone. A `match` that is a table, one value to one value, stays a table, one clause to a line. Always below, as elm-format writes every branch, was weighed and set aside for that table: it doubles its height and keeps no short `match` on one line. The all-or-none rule is the one gofmt and Prettier keep for a list of arguments. §11.6 states it when it is built, with the formatter, so that the report never states a layout the formatter does not write.
