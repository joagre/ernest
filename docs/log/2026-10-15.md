# The decisions log, 2026-10-15

## The Rationale Is a Synthesis, 2026-10-15

Decided with the user on 2026-10-15. The README's and the guide's statement of what Ernest adds had four bullets, two of which MVP 3.26 removes, checked replies and purity in the type, and one of which MVP 3.2 had made false, that code does not cross between nodes. The statement is now a synthesis: Ernest takes Erlang's processes, the types of ML as Gleam brought them to that runtime, and Unison's code known by the hash of its definition, and what it adds is where they meet, in four bullets held equal between the README and the guide by the test that already held the old ones.

The four. The mailbox in the function's type, the one claim about processes a type system cannot do without, which the first refusal of the guide's §0 shows.

Code known by its hash, which gives both a module's many versions and a spawn that carries code to a peer that lacks it; placement by room is folded into it as its payoff, since any node can then run anything, where the earlier draft had it as a piece of its own.

A distribution built for a WAN, in those words, since peers listed by key, one TLS connection opened when needed, no mesh and no port mapper make two nodes on two networks the ordinary case, and no other language on the runtime makes that claim.

And little else, two concepts, five principles, one way for each thing, a report of three files: not a feature beside the other three but what lets four parents meet without a sprawl.

The credits are exact: Hindley-Milner inference is ML's, and what was taken from Gleam is syntax and the proof that the types fit the runtime; the hash is a direction taken from Unison, not its model of a codebase, since Ernest keeps modules and files.

Gleam "made ordinary" the types of ML on the runtime, which is its achievement, where "brought" would have claimed a first that Alpaca holds.

The guide's §12 keeps the credit to Gleam for the surface that the old §0 paragraph carried, `fn`, `|>`, the update and foreign declarations, and says that Gleam's typed messaging is a library over the runtime's processes where in Ernest the process is the language, which is the whole of the difference and is said as such.

Unison is credited for its small core and not for the hash alone, since *Starting Point* and *Kept from Unison* record that the core was taken from it first.

The hash's lineage is told in §12 as the sources confirm it, checked on 2026-10-15. The erlang-questions post of 24 May 2011, *Why do we need modules at all?*, proposed every function in one global store under a unique name, and no hash; the guide quotes two of its lines, from the archive. It quotes two more of his from *A Few Improvements to Erlang*, the Erlang User Conference of 2012, as Wikiquote records them: modules as a classification problem, and git's checksum as the way to deal with time.

The 2014 talk *The Mess We're In* is reported by listeners to propose hashes in place of names at about its thirty-sixth minute. No transcript was found to confirm it, so the guide does not say it. Unison's own slides name Erlang among its influences, and no source has its authors naming any of these, so the guide says only that.

Unison Cloud runs hosted and, since its *bring your own cloud* of October 2025, on a user's own machines, so the guide no longer says Unison runs only on its service.

The argument lives in `guide/why.md`, beside the guide, and the questions in `guide/faq.md`, decided with the user the same day: a reader who wants the argument or an answer should not open a tutorial of three thousand lines for it. The README's front page carries the same list and proofs, held to `why.md`'s list by the test that held it to the guide's. The guide's §0, now *What the compiler refuses*, keeps its refusals and the layout of an error, which §1 to §8 lean on, and points at `why.md`; its §12 keeps its number and points at `faq.md`, since the exercises cite §13. A citation in either file names the guide, `the guide's §4.4`, since a bare section number outside the guide is the report's. The acknowledgments name Unison's three founders. "No cookie" was left out, since the host's cookie proves a node's floor.

One precedent on the runtime itself is known and not named in the list: Alpaca put a type on a process's `receive` on BEAM years before, and the acknowledgments name its author; what is Ernest's is the type on the function, and the nodes behind it.

Each bullet is followed by the program that shows it, as the guide shows every rule: the counter and its refused `send`, the counter taking its new loop as a message and spawned on a bare node, and an `ernest.conf` with a peer by name, key and address; the README's are fragments, run whole by the guide's §4.6 and §8.1.

The guide's §8.1 and §10 lose their sentence that no code crosses, which predated the exchange. The guide's §12 is trimmed to the questions a reader of the four asks, Gleam first, and its answers use the four's words. The site's Why, a proposal, is rewritten to the same four when that discussion resumes; its outline still names the two removed pieces.

## The Layout Completed, 2026-10-15

Decided with the user on 2026-10-15, the second part of *The Repository Laid Out Again*, before 0.4.0's tag.

Three applications under `erl/` are named for what they are. `typer` is `checker`, the report's word, which the glossary and `architecture.md` already used. `format` is `formatter`, a noun among `lexer`, `parser` and `emitter`. `utils` is `shared`, since it holds the three modules beneath every stage, `ern_diagnostic`, `ern_chunk` and `ern_namespace`, and `utils` is the kind of name `style.md` refuses for a value. The vendored getopt moved into `cli`, the one application that uses it. The modules keep their names.

The catalog of diagnostics moved from `test/` to `docs/diagnostics.md`. It is a document people read, which `make diagnostics` writes and a test holds to the compiler, as `docs/man/` is.

The tests' data moved under `test/fixtures/`, each directory keeping its name. `test/` now lists the code that tests and one directory of what it reads.

`docs/README.md` says what each file and directory of `docs/` is, the rule each proposal's directory already follows. `development.md`'s list of the documents moved there, so the list has one owner.

The layout's rule says that `docs/` holds every other document, where it said `docs/` is the project talking about itself. The manual pages are for users, and that phrase did not fit them.

An installation holds no source of the standard library. This reverses the sentence of *The Layout Under the Prefix* that installed it. Nothing on a user's machine reads it: the Emacs mode has no jump to a definition, `:browse` and `:doc` read interfaces and doc chunks, a fault names no source file, and the checker reads `.erc` files. The installed pages hold every doc block and every type. A function's body is for the curious, and it is in the repository, which the release's README names. "What runs is installed" then holds without exception. The installation still mirrors the clone for what it ships, and the build's `stdlib_root()` stays: in an installation that path is only compared, never opened. Two sentences of the report followed: §4.2 no longer calls the standard library's source root shipped with the toolchain, and Appendix E says its modules are written under `src/stdlib/`, where it said they ship as files there.

`tour/`, MVP 3.3's, will stand at the top beside `report/` and `guide/` as a third way into Ernest, as the plan already has it; decided the same day.

`docs/proposals/scratch/`, the copies written for one reader outside the project, is `docs/proposals/handouts/`, which says what they are. `scratch/` inside a proposal's directory is the user's untracked thinking, and one name meant two things.

## The Repository Laid Out Again, 2026-10-15

Decided with the user on 2026-10-15, before 0.4.0's tag. Six directories moved: `stdlib/`, `libs/` and `shell/` under `src/`, `emacs/` under `editors/`, and `proposals/` and `man/` under `docs/`. The rule is `development.md`'s: `src/` holds the Ernest code a release ships, `erl/` the Erlang it ships, `editors/` the editor modes, and everything else serves the repository.

The Ernest that ships now stands in one place. Its three kinds stay visible there: `src/stdlib/`, `src/libs/<name>/` and `src/shell/`. The editor modes have a directory of their own, with room for a second. The documents stand together under `docs/`, since the manual pages and the proposals are documents as the plan and the log are. `report/` and `guide/` stay at the top: they are the language, where `docs/` holds every other document.

`erl/` keeps its name. It holds the runtime and the libraries' Erlang sides as well as the toolchain, so no word for one job fits it, and it names its language. `toolchain/` names only one of its jobs. `kernel/` is OTP's application, and `core/` reads as Core Erlang, the compiler's language. `tools/` stays out of `src/`, since it ships nothing: `tools/manual.ern` runs as the repository builds.

The installation mirrors the clone, as it did. `tools/install.sh` copies the paths as they stand, and `bin/ern` finds `erl/*/ebin` and `build/` relative to its tree in a clone and in `lib/ernest` alike. So only the installer's list of paths changed, and `bin/ern` did not.

It is done before 0.4.0 because a release freezes its paths: the archive, the notes and every link into the repository carry them. MVP 3.26's sweep over the report, the guide and the library then works on the final paths, and nothing moves twice.
