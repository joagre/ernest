# The decisions log, 2026-11-17

## Twenty-Six Hosts Are the Cost, 2026-11-17

MVP 4.16's item 72, measured again on 2026-11-16 by an agent, a job at a time, and decided with the user that night.

**Measured again.** On an idle machine, `make -s all` with nothing to do took 1.38 s and 18.3 s of processor time, the median of 22 runs. It starts 26 hosts, 14 for `ern build` and 12 for `ern doc --man`. *Twenty-Seven Hosts for Nothing* counted 27. The manual's program runs only where one of ern(1)'s prerequisites is newer than the page, so that count met a make with work. The session counted 26 in a run of its own and timed five makes at 1.33 to 1.60 s.

**A job at a time.** Alone, each job's host starts in 0.11 s and 0.5 s of processor time. A pages job's own check is 0.03 s. A build's is 0.04 s for a library of one module, 0.11 s for the standard library and 0.15 s for `tools`. *Twenty-Seven Hosts for Nothing* read each root's check as in the noise. That holds for the pages jobs alone.

**Where a build's check goes.** From one trace of each build, good to about a tenth. Every source is parsed, 46 to 78 ms for the standard library and for `tools`. Each source is hashed and its `.erc` read and compared, 31 to 51 ms. Naming the modules takes about 20 ms even for a library of one module. By the agent's reading that step reads the standard library's interfaces from all 27 of its `.erc` files, which it did not time apart. The standard library's hash reads them again, 12 to 17 ms. Each source is read twice, and each module's own `.erc` twice.

**No stamp.** The plan's item had make start no job for a root whose sources have not changed. That is the stamp *Make Runs `ern build` Every Time* removed and *A Make With Nothing to Do* refused again: a second rule beside §11.1's that guesses at its answer (principle 2), and whose miss is silent (principle 3). The item cited neither entry. A stamp would hide that the build is slow, and make keeps none.

**The measure.** What counts is the wait on a normal laptop, and the machine measured has 24 threads. There the 26 hosts start side by side, and their starts hide in a wait of 1.4 s. On fewer cores they queue: *A Make With Nothing to Do* had 2.9 s for 16 hosts on eight threads. So the defect is the count of hosts. The session first dropped one host for the 26, on an estimate that summed the jobs' checks as 26 hosts run them. The probe below shows the estimate wrong on every machine.

**Decided with the user: one host.** One host does the work of the 26. It starts once and runs each root's check in turn, by §11.1's rule and no other. The check is made leaner in it: each file is read once, and the standard library's interfaces are read once where 26 hosts read them 26 times.

**Probed.** By the session, with a scratch module that runs the 26 jobs' command lines in turn through `ern_cli:ern/2` in one host, three runs each way, pinned to fewer threads by `taskset`. On 24 threads make took 1.35 to 1.43 s and the one host 0.52 s. On 8 threads they took 1.39 to 1.45 s and 0.46 s, on 4 threads 2.06 to 2.11 s and 0.46 s, and on 2 threads 3.41 to 3.45 s and 0.52 to 0.54 s. On 24 threads the one host spent 1.5 s of processor time, where make spends 18 s.

The 26 jobs take 0.41 s inside the one host, where their checks sum to about 1.4 s in hosts of their own, since the host loads its code once. Each job ended with status 0 and wrote the lines it writes under make. The one host's times leave out make's own 0.13 s. The pinned cores are this machine's, so a slower machine's times are longer.
**On two old machines.** The same makes and the same probe, by the session, three runs each, after a pull and a build. On xena, four cores of 2012, make with nothing to do took 5.5 to 5.6 s and the one host 1.6 to 1.7 s. On bee, two cores, they took 16.4 s and 3.4 to 3.5 s. A bare host starts in 0.32 s on xena and 0.55 s on bee. The standard library's build with nothing to do takes 0.64 s and 1.31 s, and built whole 3.0 s and 5.6 s.

**How the one job is said.** No user needs it. Only who builds Ernest from source runs it, before any Ernest program can run, so it is Erlang. The Makefile starts one host and gives it the jobs' command lines, which it runs in turn through the command's own code. So §11.1 stays the one rule, no sentence of the report changes, and `ern` gains no word. A host that builds more than once is not new: the shell does at each `:reload`.

Several roots on one command line stay refused, as *The Tools Measured* had it, since a root package is how a user says them (principle 2). §11.9's build compiles several roots in one host, but the toolchain's roots are no packages: an entry at the namespace of a library of the toolchain's own is refused, the standard library provides §4.2's namespaces and not one name's, and the libraries build into `build/libs`.

**What the one host leaves.** Inside it the 26 checks take 0.41 s on nike, 1.3 s on xena and 2.8 s on bee. That is the parse of every source and the double reads, so the leaner check is the item's second build.

Two costs stand after it. A build's work runs in one process: the standard library built whole takes 5.6 s on one of bee's two cores. And every call pays its host's start and its code's loading, 0.55 s of bee's 1.31 s for a build with nothing to do. A host that compiles side by side and one that stays between calls are a compile server's two halves, and go to a proposal. The plan's item 89 holds where it stands.

**What it cost.** The agent took 21 minutes, 4 of them the machine's. The session's probe took a minute on nike, and the two old machines' runs 2 and 4 minutes.

## The Busy Wait Stays, 2026-11-17

MVP 4.16's item 82, measured on 2026-11-16 by an agent and closed with the user that night. The variants are the host as `ern` starts it, the same with the schedulers' busy wait off, and, for a job that runs no program, one scheduler of each kind.

**A program that does nothing.** Under `ern run`, alone, it took 0.218 s as now and 0.227 s with the busy wait off, the same to within 8 ms, and 1.21 s of processor time against 0.33 s. Beside the flake hunt it took 1.22 s against 0.80 s. Beside 24 busy loops it took 8.6 s against 1.5 s.

**A build.** Its work runs in one process, as does that of every job that runs no program. The standard library's build with nothing to do, alone, took 0.244 s as now, 0.275 s with the busy wait off and 0.277 s with one scheduler. Built whole it took 0.931 s, 0.979 s and 1.025 s, in 2.45 s, 1.11 s and 1.18 s of processor time.

`make -s all` with nothing to do turns the other way: 1.44 s, 1.25 s and 1.14 s, in 18.8 s, 8.9 s and 7.6 s of processor time. The session timed five makes each way at 1.48 to 1.58 s, 1.27 to 1.32 s and 1.12 to 1.22 s.

**A running program.** `make bench` ran three times each way, in 446 s as now and 467 s with the busy wait off. A column counts as moved where every run one way lies beyond every run the other way, by more than the runs' own spread and by 3 %. Of 627 columns 31 moved, where chance gives about 11.

With the busy wait off the host's own call with one caller is 38 % slower and `Address.call` 14 to 16 %. A spawn's steps in the code table are 46 to 66 % slower, and a call to this node's process takes 2.0 µs where it took 1.6. The tables of bursts, spawns, monitors, restarts and the language's constructs did not move.

**Decided with the user: the busy wait stays.** The plan asked whether the launcher turns it off. Off, every call of a running program pays for a start, and what Ernest adds to a host operation costs a fraction of it (CLAUDE.md's *Design*).

**One scheduler for a job that runs no program, dropped.** Its work is one process's, the argument of *The First Start's Schedulers*. But alone the job is slower, a build by 19 ms with nothing to do and by 8 % with work, and one build alone is the commonest case. What it gained was among the hosts a make starts side by side, and item 72's one host takes those away.

**What is left as it is.** A job that runs a program starts in 8.6 s beside a load on every processor, a load no target runs. A second measurement, of a host that boots with one scheduler online, was stopped unfinished: its series ran on 24 threads, and its number decided nothing once the measure was the wait on a normal laptop.

**What it cost.** The first measurement took 1 h 30 min, 46 minutes of it six runs of the bench, where two short runs of the call table would have decided the busy wait. No brief stated the decision its numbers served or a bound in time, the mistake *Two Hours for a Hunt* records for a hunt.
