## A Node's End Waits for No Send, 2026-11-20

MVP 4.16's item 124, decided with the user: a node's end waits for no message and no `Down` that still waits to be placed, and §8.7 says so. No code changed. The same entry holds what item 118's showing with a still peer found, mended by narrower sentences too.

**The setting.** One agent made 37 runs on three nodes over loopback, in item 121's setting (*A Down Follows the Send*): a desk whose sender waits in a send of 3, 8 or 28 MB to a store that stands still, stopped or its connection held back, with watchers on the store and on a third node. The desk's program ends while the sender waits.

**The cause.** The plan's reading holds. The host says each process of the desk is gone 2 to 6 ms after the end, 37 runs of 37, while the sender's exit still waits to place the rest of its message and its `Down`s for other nodes. The runtime's end waits for the host's word, so `ern_carrier:depart/0` begins at once, and its frame to the store waits at the same busy buffer. Once the store takes again, the frame crosses before a large rest does, the store answers the host's question, and the node halts.

**What decides.** The size of the rest alone. The store took the message whole in 8 runs of 8 at 3 MB, 0 of 3 at 8 MB and 2 of 24 at 28 MB. How long the store stood still did not matter, from nothing to 30 s, nor whether it was stopped or held. Past the loss, the loss dropped the rest, 2 runs of 2.

**What the peers are told.** Where the rest was cut, the store took none of the message and its watcher was told `Unreachable`, 25 runs of 25, and the third's in 23. In the other two the exit had placed everything behind the frame: the third was told `ProgramEnd`, and the store's part was cut in the host's buffer. Where the rest crossed first, both watchers were told the end's own reason, 10 runs of 10. Both peers said `ended` in every run. A process that waited in no send gave `ProgramEnd` at once, 37 of 37.

**The fault is no case of its own.** The plan had the runtime's fault for a standard input that is not UTF-8 give `Unreachable` and never `Fault`. Its reason is a tuple and waits behind the rest as `ProgramEnd`'s does: `Unreachable` at 28 MB, 4 runs of 4, and `Fault` with the message whole at 3 MB, 4 of 4.

**Decided: the narrower sentence.** Principle 1 decides. Its reader knows processes as Erlang has them, and predicts that a node which stops in the middle of a send loses the message and that its watchers hear of a connection gone. The sentence is true of every end, and a program that needs its message across waits for an answer to it before it ends.

**Set aside: the end waits for those exits.** The host gives one sign that an exit has placed its message and its `Down`s, the trace event `out_exited`, which came only after the store took again and, where it came before the frame reached the store, with the message across, 10 runs of 10. An end that traced each process it kills and sent its frame after that event would add the rest's placing, 4 to 22 ms here, bounded by the loss. It was read and not built. It puts the host's tracing into every node's end, a call for each process alive then. And it leaves the hole open: a process killed or faulted just before the end is listed by the host no more while its exit still places, so the end cannot find it, and the sentence would be needed beside the mechanism. No other sign was found: the local `'DOWN'`, `processes()` and `Process.status` say gone at once. A hole in a guarantee is answered first by the narrower promise, and this one is not planned for MVP 4.5.

**The argument against.** On 2026-10-10 a node's end became no loss to its peers, so that a peer tells an end from a crash. This lets a loss's reason through for a process that waited in a send. What stays: the peers' own lines say `ended`, and every other process gives `ProgramEnd`.

**The sentences.** §8.7's *Messages* says of any death what it said of `Killed`: a process that dies while what it sends waits to be placed has sent it, until a loss or the node's end. A new paragraph says that the word that a node ends is sent as a `send` sends, that what is placed for the peer before it crosses before it, that the end waits for nothing else, and that what is not placed by then may be dropped, a watcher told `Unreachable` and a call ended as at a loss. "A message is never dropped alone" names the node's end beside the loss. *Its start and its end* has the node tell each peer after what is placed for it, where it had every `Down` already on its way cross, and a monitor give `ProgramEnd`, or `Unreachable` where its `Down` was dropped. §6.9 and §9.3 allow `Unreachable` so, §12.6 follows, and §8.6's copy of the first sentence is a pointer.

**Why "may".** The report promises what crosses, what was placed before the node's word, and says "may" of the rest, as *A Down Follows the Send* argued for a `Down`'s wait. Which side a run takes is the host's order of resuming what waits at a buffer, which was not traced.

**Item 118's showing.** A fourth node, listed and not connected, dialed the desk while a still store held it between its processes' deaths and its halt, 3 runs of 3. The connection opened. Two sentences more, each after the rule §11.2 has for a node between two runs of `ern test`: a find, a spawn, a compute or a utilization that reaches a node while it stops answers `Unreachable`, and Appendix E.1's meaning of `Unreachable` gains both cases, and `Peer.compute` with no utilization to use, which E.27 had and E.1 lacked.

## The Disconnect Waits for Nothing, 2026-11-20

MVP 4.16's item 125: the three sites that end a connection call the host's `net_kernel:async_disconnect/1`, as *What a Peer's Worker Waits On* decided. §12.5 gains one sentence, decided with the user, and `docs/otp_bugs.md` has report 26.

**The gateway's refusal.** The gateway's own disconnect waited for the refused connection's process, and the gateway stood with it: no utilization was answered for 30 s, 2 runs of 2. Every peer's frames waited behind it. No other site's hold reaches the gateway, since no connection comes up while the host's process of the connections is held. That was read and not shown for a `nodeup` already queued.

**The sighting.** It was the disconnect's. Before the fix a quiet peer said that the node was lost, `it fell silent`, 74.7 s after the node's program ended, 3 runs of 3. The held process passed no tick, and the node's tellings had stopped at its end. After the fix the peer said that the node ended, when it halted 84 s later, 3 runs of 3: the node's end waited on the stopped peer's loss, as §8.7 has it.

**A stopped peer's loss.** After a reload removed a stopped peer whose connection was full, the node ran its loss 84.1 to 84.2 s later, 5 runs of 5, with `it closed`. That is the host's time for such a connection (*A Connection That Cannot Drain*), which §12.6 states as the host's.

**The window after a reload.** `erlang:disconnect_node/1` answered once the connection was down, and the cast answers at once. So the node now says that a peer was removed before the connection has ended, where it said so after. Until the end the removed peer is served: a spawn it sent after the line ran on the node, its connection held back, 3 runs of 3. The window was there before the fix, behind the line. By a reading of the host's tick, which gives up only a peer from which nothing comes, a peer that keeps sending and reads nothing keeps its connection without limit. That was not shown. Before the fix such a peer held the whole node.

No other site rested on the end. A frame that comes between the cast and the end is read as it was read before, and a refused peer's frames reach the gateway alone.

**Decided: a sentence now, and a guard after the launch.** §12.5 was silent on when the connection ends. Principle 1 decides, as it did in *A Connection That Cannot Drain*: the reader knows processes as Erlang has them, and a connection ends when the host says so. The sentence is too weak only against the peer that sends and reads nothing. That peer is built on purpose, and could start code on the node while it was listed, so by *The Line Before the Launch* its mechanism is MVP 4.5's item 144: from the reload on, the gateway drops the runtime's frames of a node no longer listed. What the host carries itself would still cross until the close, so the sentence is needed with the guard too.

Set aside again: killing the connection's process, which rests on how the host's TLS is built.

The line now ends `and this node ends its connection`, which is §12.6's own sentence and true when it is said. Principle 1 decides: an operator who reads `was ended` predicts that the peer is gone. The line stays before the cast, so that it comes before the loss line it causes. Set aside: the line said at the loss, which is true and up to 85 s late.

## A Worker Waits on No Third Node, 2026-11-20

**The `find`'s alias.** The `find` frame takes the `compute` frame's guard, so a `find` that names an alias of another node than its sender's is a frame the node cannot read, and the node ends the connection (§12.6).

**The tests.** `worker_waits_on_no_third_node` is the reading's `kept` probe made a test of `ern_nodes_tests`. **The note and its test.** The nodes' note names the worker's one send by its function, and `unwaiting_processes_test` holds that name to the code beside the three processes.
