MVP 3.0's item 11, CLAUDE.md's *No fixed sleep* carried through the tests. The plan counted 33 sleeps in ten files; the inventory found about a hundred in sixteen: the direct calls, the wrappers `nap` and `sleep` and their calls, the polls with a pause between asks, the pauses in the Ernest programs the tests run, the terminal harness's `sleep:` step and its own polls, and the nine polls items 4 to 10 had added.

- **A sleep that stood for an event waits on the event**: a message, a monitor's `Down`, a fault's report, a line written, a call answered. What only the host reports, `erl/runtime/test/ern_waits.erl` reads by tracing: a process blocked in its receive or running, read at each time it is scheduled out; a function called or returned from, the reaper's looks among them; and, by monitors, the deliveries to a process ended. An Ernest program under test reaches them through a `foreign fn`.
- **A poll became what it polled, as a message**: a node's output through named pipes that `tee` reads into its file and hands to a watcher; the journal followed; a program's line read from its port. The terminal harness waits on output and on the child's end, which `SIGCHLD` makes an event, and its `put` step opens a named pipe a program waits on, where a fault must come while a line is typed.
- **An absence waits a time derived from what it waits for**, with the reason beside it: twice a timer's milliseconds, a killed program's own second from the moment it was killed, twice the time the same writes take when read, three times what a plain run takes to find its deadlock. Where the absent thing would be a delivery, the deliveries already started are waited for instead, and each such test says that one the reaper had not yet started is not seen.
- **A time that is a test's subject is derived from it**: a call's own time, waited from within the call, whose clock has started; a restart limit's window, its faults half a window and a quarter window from its alarms. A time that bounds a failure stays, and is said to be one.
- **What the sleeps hid were the tests' own beliefs.** A test's comment said a restart keeps the mailbox, which §6.9 says it empties; the supervisor's and the restart's tests sent a message or made a `callForever` before a restart had finished, which a pause let pass; the comment is corrected, and a call made around a restart is made again where it ends (`askUntil`). No defect of the runtime was found: each failure a removed sleep showed was the test's.
- **The one chosen time left** is the load harness's 100 ms after it collects every process: the host frees a collected heap's blocks later, on the scheduler that made them, and says nothing; measured, the shell's load varied by 1.2 MB without it and by about 15 KB with it.
- **The toolchain's own.** `ern_rt`'s and `ern_out`'s flush ask a port's queue every millisecond until it is empty or the port has ended, its reader gone, which §11 ends with 141. The host answers a port's close once what the port holds is written, 620 ms behind a reader that slept a second with all 1,000,000 bytes taken, and a close leaves the descriptor open, so a flush that closed the port and opened the descriptor again was built; but the host answers a close the same way where the reader had gone, a `normal` `DOWN` and then `closed`, and the tests of a stream lost at the end saw 0 for 141. So the poll stays, at the host's finest wait, the one poll of the toolchain's own: nothing the host reports shows a port's queue drained. `ern_tty`'s two seconds bound an `stty` that does not finish. `ern_tcp`'s closer looks at what the host still holds once a second: the host tells nothing as its send queue drains either, and the look serves E.18's five seconds without progress, at a fifth of them.
