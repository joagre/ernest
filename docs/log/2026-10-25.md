## The Teller's Late Delivery, Built, 2026-10-25

MVP 3.95's item 12, built the day it was decided (*The Teller's Late Delivery*).

**The check.** A teller's delivery reads its subscriber's row in `ern_terminating` once the teller has written the delivery's own row and let it go. It delivers through the address the row holds where the row holds the end's telling. Where the row holds another telling or none, it delivers nothing. It then tells its teller that the subscriber restarted, where the subscriber lives, since a restart that read its callers before the teller's call was noted tells the teller nothing. Where the subscriber has died, the teller's monitor of it tells the teller, as before.

**Why the check is exact.** The reaper takes a restart in one order: it deletes the subscription's row, reads the deliveries to the process and ends each, and answers. The delivery's row is written before the delivery reads the subscription's. So either the reaper's read finds the delivery's row and the delivery ends with the old run, or the row was written after that read began, so after the subscription went, and the delivery finds no row or the new run's. A row that still holds the telling is one no restart has deleted, and the restart that deletes it finds the delivery's row.

**Two things the decision left to the build.** `Told` holds the telling's reference, not `true`. A boolean is not exact: the old teller can have its word from the restart before its delivery reads, the runner then tells the new run's subscription, and a delivery reading `true` would take that telling for its own. The delivery also takes the address from the row, not the one the end read. The end's mark can land on a row the new run wrote between the end's read and the mark, and the address told is then that run's own. Within one run it is the latest subscription's, which is what E.23 says a subscription is.

**The test.** `teller_after_the_restart_test` in `ern_rt_tests` places the race, which the clock's could not be. The reaper is held suspended while the subscriber's restart asks it and the termination is told, so the teller's first ask comes after the restart's in the reaper's mailbox, and its delivery's row after the reaper's read. Under the old code the delivery applied the wrap in the new run, which held the reply and did not answer, and the end waited until the test's timeout, in five runs of five; the restart had read its callers before the teller's call was noted. Under the new code the test passed 300 runs of 300. The check costs one lookup of a row for each subscriber the end tells.
